## 1. THE CONFIRMED-VS-WATCHING PARADOX (highest priority — investigate first)

**The anomaly:** RSI calibration data shows a clean, monotonic relationship — tighter RSI cutoff, better win rate:
- RSI≤35 → 60% WR
- RSI≤40 → 57% WR
- RSI≤50 → 54% WR (this is the WATCHING threshold)

CONFIRMED uses RSI≤45 — tighter than WATCHING's ≤50. By the pattern above, CONFIRMED should land around 55-56% WR, comfortably beating WATCHING's 54%.

Instead, actual backtest results are:
- CONFIRMED: n=4,384, WR=44.6%, FP=88.5%
- WATCHING: n=29,194, WR=50.8%, FP=74.0%

CONFIRMED is worse than WATCHING on BOTH win rate and false-positive rate — a 10-point WR gap in the wrong direction from what the RSI relationship alone predicts.

**Questions to ask:**
- "List every single condition that differs between CONFIRMED state and WATCHING state — not just the RSI threshold. What else changes?"
- "For each of those other conditions individually, run a query showing win rate broken down by that condition alone, holding RSI constant. Which one is dragging CONFIRMED's win rate down?"
- "Is it possible CONFIRMED is requiring EXPANDING volume or a specific support_level_type that's a worse predictor, and that's riding along inside the CONFIRMED label without being isolated?"
- "Pull 20 real CONFIRMED rows and 20 real WATCHING rows side by side, all columns, and show me where they actually differ beyond the RSI cutoff."

**Do not accept:** "CONFIRMED is stricter so it should be better" as an answer — that's the assumption being violated. Require the actual breakdown.
