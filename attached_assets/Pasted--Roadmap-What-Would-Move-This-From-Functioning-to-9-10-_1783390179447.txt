## Roadmap — What Would Move This From "Functioning" to "9/10 Institutional-Grade"

**Important distinction:** everything above this line is about verifying what you've already been told is built. Everything below is a **future build list**, not a verification target. Do not let this section substitute for finishing the verification above — a system with more features bolted onto an unproven learning loop is not more trustworthy, it's just more surface area to eventually verify.

### 1. Reinforcement learning that actually changes behavior over time (biggest gap)
Confirmed empty as of the last audit: Thompson sampler / trust weights / RL buffer = INCOMPLETE. Infrastructure existing is not the same as the adaptive logic being operational. A working version would show:
- Confidence scores changing over time
- Strategy weights adapting
- Modules gaining or losing influence based on performance
- Measurable improvement after learning cycles — not just claimed, shown

### 2. Position sizing as a first-class decision
A hedge-fund-quality system doesn't just decide *what* to trade — it decides:
- How much capital to allocate
- Maximum position size
- Risk budget per trade
- Correlation limits across open positions
- Slippage and execution modeling

### 3. Automatic strategy retirement and promotion
Weak signals should lose influence or get retired automatically; strong discoveries should get promoted — with a retained history of how each signal's quality changed over time, without manual intervention.

### 4. Portfolio-level optimization
Beyond single-trade decisions: how positions interact, combined risk exposure, and capital allocation across the whole book — not just ticker-by-ticker.

### 5. Walk-forward validation (stronger than current out-of-sample testing)
You already have out-of-sample testing, which is good. A stronger setup repeatedly retrains and re-tests across many rolling time windows instead of relying on a single train/test split.

### 6. Long-term adaptive monitoring
You already have drift checks. A more mature version would also:
- Automatically retire weak signals
- Reduce their influence gradually rather than binary on/off
- Promote stronger discoveries
- Keep a running history of how signal quality changes over time
- All without manual intervention

### 7. Better uncertainty calibration
Instead of a bare directional call ("Buy"), a more mature output includes:
- A confidence percentage (e.g. 62% confidence)
- Expected return
- Downside risk
- Confidence interval
- Historical reliability of this setup under similar past conditions

This gives you conviction, not just direction — useful for sizing and for deciding whether to act on a signal at all.

### How to use this section
Treat items 1–7 as a backlog, prioritized in the order listed — RL/learning-loop first, since everything else (sizing, retirement, calibration) is more valuable once you know the system is actually learning rather than just logging. Before starting on any of these, get a hard yes/no with raw proof on whether the RL/Thompson-sampling loop is wired at all — that answer determines whether items 2–7 are worth building yet or would just be more unverified surface area on top of an unproven core.
