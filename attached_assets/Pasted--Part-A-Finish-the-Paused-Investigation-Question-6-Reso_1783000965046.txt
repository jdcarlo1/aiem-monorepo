# Part B — Wiring a Real Feedback Loop (loopback: saved data → self-improvement)

## Context / what currently exists (confirmed so far)
- `aiem_signal_discoveries` stores validated findings but nothing reads
  them back to change scoring, weights, or future scan rules.
- A separate `retrain_pipeline.py` / `automated_retrain_pipeline.py`
  exists and retrains on settled trade outcomes from `ai_short_calls_log`
  — but has zero connection to `aiem_signal_discoveries`.
- `signal_trust_weights` + `meta_learning_signal_trust.py` may be a real,
  wired mechanism — pending Question 6 above.
- No mechanism currently lets the 24/7 research loop look back at its own
  past discoveries and use them to change future behavior.

## Goal
Build/wire a real loopback: the system saves discoveries → later reads its
own saved data → uses it to measurably change future scanning/scoring
behavior. Not just accumulate more validated rows.

## Question 7 — Design decision needed before building

Instruction to give the agent (this is a design question, answer in plain
language before writing code):

> Before building anything, propose a concrete design for closing the
> loop between aiem_signal_discoveries and future behavior. Address:
>
> 1. **What should a validated discovery actually change?** Options:
>    (a) get added as a new scan rule/indicator combo for future grid
>    battery runs, (b) adjust a weight/confidence multiplier in the
>    9-layer conviction stack, (c) both.
> 2. **What triggers the update?** A scheduled job (e.g. weekly, like the
>    existing auto-retire job) or immediately when a discovery clears the
>    gates?
> 3. **Does this require human approval before going live**, or should it
>    be fully automatic? (Recommend requiring approval initially — an
>    automatic system that mis-promotes a false-positive pattern into live
>    scanning could actively hurt results, not just fail to help.)
> 4. **How do we measure "improvement"?** Propose a concrete metric
>    (e.g., win rate of newly-promoted combos vs. the original baseline
>    combos, tracked over N weeks) so we can later verify this is actually
>    working, not just running.
