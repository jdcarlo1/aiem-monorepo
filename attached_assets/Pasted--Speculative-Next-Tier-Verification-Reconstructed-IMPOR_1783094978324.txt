# Speculative Next-Tier Verification (Reconstructed)

**IMPORTANT — This is NOT a recovered copy of a real file.** The original "ultimate test" file that came after Advanced Stress Verification was lost, and its exact contents are unknown. This document is a reasoned reconstruction, based on the escalation pattern of the prior tiers (wiring → concurrency/adversarial → statistical soundness → soak test → security), of what a genuinely harder tier would logically need to cover. Treat it as a draft to review and edit, not as ground truth of what was previously sent.

## 1. Chaos / Infrastructure Failure Injection

Simulate real infrastructure failures rather than just code-level mid-write kills: Polygon/Tradier/Finnhub returning malformed or delayed data mid-cycle, Replit hosting restarting unexpectedly, DB connection pool exhaustion under load, and network partition between modules. Proof required: the system fails safe or recovers cleanly, not silently degrades or produces corrupted state.

## 2. Adversarial Market Conditions

Feed the system historical extreme-volatility data (e.g. May 2010 flash crash, February 2018 VIX spike, March 2020 COVID crash) or synthetic tail-risk scenarios. Check whether kill_switch trips fast enough, whether gamma/charm calculations remain stable at extreme values, and whether portfolio_correlation_risk correctly flags a scenario where most positions move together. Proof required: logs from a replay run showing gate behavior under stress, not a theoretical description.

## 3. Model / Data Drift Detection

A system statistically sound at build time can silently decay as live conditions diverge from backtest assumptions. Ask whether anything monitors live performance of the 9-layer conviction stack against its original validation baseline, and whether there is automated alerting for drift, or whether detection depends entirely on someone manually re-running validation. Proof required: evidence of a drift-monitoring mechanism, or an explicit admission that none exists.

## 4. Adversarial Agent / Injection Resistance

Because AIEM includes an autonomous research loop and multi-agent debate (Agent Lab), test whether malformed or adversarial inputs (ticker names, news text, agent-generated content) can manipulate agent reasoning or reach a downstream execution or SQL path. This goes beyond the SQL-injection check already covered in the prior security section — it targets the agents themselves, not just raw queries.

## 5. Tamper-Evidence Under an Attacker Model

HMAC provenance signing proves data wasn't altered after the fact, assuming the signing key itself is secure. Ask whether any downstream process or compromised credential could forge a valid-looking signed record, and whether the signing key has a single point of failure.

## 6. Independent Cross-Implementation Check

Reimplement one or two of the highest-stakes layers (e.g., gamma or charm) completely independently, outside the existing codebase, and diff the outputs against the live system over a sample period. This is the only method in the whole audit stack that can catch a bug that has been consistently wrong since day one and therefore looks correct in every internal test, because internal tests share the same underlying assumptions as the code being tested.

## 7. Regulatory / Compliance Surface

If real capital is ever involved, or the product is ever exposed to other users: pattern day trader rule handling, wash sale tracking, and whether any "signal discovery" language could be construed as investment advice.

## Note on Priority

Items 1, 3, and 6 are the ones most likely to catch a real problem that the other tiers structurally cannot — they don't assume the existing code is basically correct, and they test against reality (infrastructure, live drift, and an independent reimplementation) rather than against the system's own internal logic.
