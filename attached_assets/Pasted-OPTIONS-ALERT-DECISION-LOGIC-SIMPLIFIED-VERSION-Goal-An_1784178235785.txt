OPTIONS ALERT DECISION LOGIC — SIMPLIFIED VERSION

Goal:
Analyze the stock and options market, determine whether the opportunity is bullish or bearish, and compare only a long call versus a long put. Do not build or compare multi-leg options strategies yet.

Before sending an alert, evaluate:

1. UNDERLYING STOCK ANALYSIS
- Current trend and market structure
- Support and resistance
- VWAP position and VWAP reclaim/rejection
- Relative volume and volume confirmation
- Momentum and momentum exhaustion
- Breakout, breakdown, pullback, or reversal setup
- Broader market direction
- Sector strength or weakness
- Market breadth
- Volatility regime
- Existing AEIM probability and conviction scores

2. OPTIONS CONTRACT ANALYSIS
- Call volume versus put volume
- Volume compared with open interest
- Open-interest change when available
- Unusual options activity
- Sweep/block activity when available
- Bid/ask spread
- Contract liquidity
- Slippage estimate
- Implied volatility
- IV rank and IV percentile
- Delta
- Gamma
- Theta
- Vega
- Gamma exposure and dealer-positioning context
- Put/call skew
- Expected move
- Strike selection
- Expiration selection
- Break-even price
- Premium at risk
- Probability of profit or probability of reaching the target
- Expected return
- Maximum possible loss
- Time remaining before expiration

3. LIMITED DIRECTIONAL COMPARISON
Compare only:
- Best eligible long call
- Best eligible long put
- No trade

Do not compare spreads, calendars, condors, butterflies, straddles, strangles, ratio spreads, covered calls, collars, or synthetic positions at this stage.

4. SCORING
Score the best call and best put using:
- Directional probability
- Expected return
- Maximum loss
- Risk/reward
- Liquidity
- Bid/ask spread
- Estimated slippage
- Theta-decay risk
- IV-expansion or IV-crush risk
- Capital required
- Market-regime fit
- Technical confirmation
- Options-flow confirmation
- Historical performance in similar conditions

5. ALERT GATE
Send an alert only when:
- The underlying stock signal and options signal agree
- Liquidity meets the minimum threshold
- Bid/ask spread is acceptable
- Expected return justifies the premium at risk
- The contract has enough time before expiration
- The selected direction clearly scores higher than the opposite direction
- The total score exceeds the minimum alert threshold
- No major conflicting signal invalidates the setup

Otherwise return NO TRADE.

6. REQUIRED ALERT OUTPUT
- Ticker
- Bullish, bearish, or no trade
- Selected contract
- Call or put
- Strike
- Expiration
- Current stock price
- Entry premium range
- Delta, gamma, theta, vega, and IV
- Volume
- Open interest
- Bid/ask spread
- Expected move
- Break-even
- Maximum premium at risk
- Probability estimate
- Expected-return estimate
- Suggested profit target
- Suggested stop or invalidation level
- Main reasons for the alert
- Main risks
- Score for the selected contract
- Score for the rejected opposite-direction contract
- Exact reason the selected contract won

The system must never force an alert. NO TRADE is required whenever neither the long call nor long put meets the minimum quality, liquidity, and capital-preservation standards.
