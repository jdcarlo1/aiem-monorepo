# Pool Deadlock Investigation — psycopg2.connect monkeypatch

Root cause hypothesis: `psycopg2.connect` has been monkeypatched to point at
`_pg_pooled_connect()` itself. When the pool is empty, `ThreadedConnectionPool._connect()`
calls `psycopg2.connect(...)` to open a fresh raw connection — but that call gets
redirected back into `_pg_pooled_connect()`, which calls `_PG_POOL.getconn()` again
on the same thread that already holds the (non-reentrant) `self._lock`. Permanent
self-deadlock.


2. **Is the pool constructed with a `connection_factory` argument?**

3. **After removing/fixing the patch, prove the deadlock is gone:**

   - Paste a `py-spy dump` (or equivalent thread dump) taken *during* that load test
     showing no thread blocked inside `self._lock.acquire()` at `psycopg2/pool.py:167`
     for more than a few ms.

## Verification required before closing — fix must reproduce the actual trigger condition

The original deadlock only fires when the pool tries to **grow past its pre-warmed
connections while multiple concurrent callers** hit it at once (that's what happened
at T+180s in the outage — concurrent morning scans forcing the pool past its 2
pre-warmed connections). Sequential health-check pings against an idle pool will
pass every time whether or not the bug is actually fixed. "28/28 endpoints green"
and "zero threads blocked" as a static summary does NOT prove this.

1. **Fire 5–10 concurrent requests at once** (not sequential) against an endpoint
   that needs a DB connection, starting from a cold/idle pool at 2 connections —
   forcing it to grow past pre-warmed capacity.
2. **Capture a thread dump (`py-spy dump`) during that burst**, not after it
   completes.
3. Paste the dump and confirm zero threads sitting in `self._lock.acquire()` at
   `psycopg2/pool.py:167` for more than a few ms. A claim without the dump doesn't
   count as proof.
