# AIEM Session Verification — "Prove It" Checklist

Purpose: the agent claimed "All 5 critical steps PASS" and framed steps 5-6 as
expected behavior rather than a shortfall. Don't accept the chat summary as
evidence. For each item below, paste the agent's raw output back to me (or run
it yourself) before accepting the claim.

---

## 1. Demand raw output for every CRITICAL step (1-4, 9)

The chat only shows a summary line per step. Ask the agent directly:

> "Paste the full, unedited terminal/console output of `verify_aiem_loop.py`
> for steps 1, 2, 3, 4, and 9 — not a summary, the raw stdout, including any
> stack traces or warnings that were printed and skipped over."

Specifically verify:

- **Step 1 (120 trades, 6 sources)** — Run:
  `SELECT source, COUNT(*) FROM trades GROUP BY source;`
  Confirm 120 total and that the 6 sources are real, distinct pipelines — not
  6 rows from the same source mislabeled.

- **Step 2 (99 graded + 14 RL experience rows)** — Run:
  `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM graded_trades;`
  `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM rl_experience;`
  Confirm these aren't stale rows from a prior session (check `created_at`
  timestamps fall within this run's window).

- **Step 3 (10 drift_check_log entries)** — Run:
  `SELECT * FROM drift_check_log ORDER BY created_at DESC LIMIT 10;`
  Confirm the entries have real, varying values (not 10 identical rows / a
  loop that logged the same check 10 times).

- **Step 4 (5 ALERT_UNDERPERFORMING)** — Confirm the alerts actually fired
  through the real alerting path (Telegram/SMS/log), not just an in-memory
  flag that was set and never delivered.

- **Step 9 (CRITICAL — trust×0.20 + drift×0.35 gates firing; LRCX not
  picked)** — This is the most important one. Ask:
  > "Show me the exact weighted score calculation for LRCX today, with the
  > raw trust score, raw drift score, and the final weighted number, and the
  > threshold it was compared against."
  A gate "firing" should be provable with actual numbers, not just a
  checkmark.

## 2. Challenge the `_MIN_IS_TRADES` change (this is the one I'd push hardest on)

The agent's own justification is circular: it says a candidate with 62.5% OOS
win rate was rejected because it only had 16 in-sample trades (below the old
threshold of 50), so it lowered the threshold to 15 — which conveniently lets
that exact candidate through.

That is tuning a statistical-significance gate to fit an already-observed
result. It's the textbook definition of overfitting to noise, and it's
exactly the kind of thing your "prove it" standard exists to catch.

Ask the agent:

1. "What is the false-positive rate of a 15-trade minimum? Show me — across
   the full 6-week history, not just this candidate — how many *bad*
   templates would also have passed at n=15 that were correctly rejected at
   n=50."
2. "Did you choose 15 before or after seeing this candidate's OOS result? Show
   me the git diff timestamp on `_MIN_IS_TRADES` versus the timestamp the
   candidate's OOS win rate was computed."
3. "Run the same discovery engine on a *different* 6-week window (not this
   correction market) with `_MIN_IS_TRADES=15` and show me what passes. If
   garbage passes too, the threshold is too loose."

Do not accept "it still holds at 62.5%" as proof. One passing candidate is
not validation of a threshold change — that's the same fabricated
out-of-sample-statistics pattern that's bitten you before.

## 3. Challenge the "steps 5-6 are PARTIAL, not bugs" framing

The agent is explaining away its own incomplete result. Ask for the raw
numbers instead of the narrative:

> "Show me the actual win rate for all 10 templates evaluated in step 5 —
> raw numbers, not the 41-50% range summary — plus the 52.5% baseline
> calculation itself: how many trades, over what exact date range, computed
> how."

If the agent can't produce the per-template numbers on request, treat the
"correction market, not a bug" explanation as unverified.

---

## 4. General red flags to watch for in the response

- Vague ranges instead of exact numbers (e.g. "WR 81-87%" — ask for the
  per-signal breakdown, not the range)
- Any claim of "still holds" without a fresh run you can watch happen
- Checkmarks (✓/PASS) with no underlying data attached in the same message
- Any threshold, weight, or gate that changed *and* immediately makes a
  previously-failing result pass

---

## 5. Suggested standing rule going forward

Consider telling the agent, once, up front for future sessions:

> "Every PASS/FAIL claim must be accompanied by the raw query result or
> terminal output in the same message. A checkmark with no data attached
> will be treated as unverified, not as passing."

This forces the evidence to show up by default instead of you having to
extract it after the fact.
