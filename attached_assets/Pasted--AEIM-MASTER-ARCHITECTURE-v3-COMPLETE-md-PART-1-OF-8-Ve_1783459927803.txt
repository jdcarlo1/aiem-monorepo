# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This document defines the complete architecture of the AEIM
(Autonomous Market Intelligence Engine).

The goal is NOT simply to create another stock screener.

The goal is to create an autonomous market intelligence system that
continuously improves its own ability to discover opportunities,
evaluate evidence, manage risk, learn from experience, and adapt to
changing market environments.

This document supersedes previous architecture files.

This is the single source of truth for AEIM.

====================================================================

# MISSION

AEIM must become an autonomous market intelligence engine.

Its responsibilities include:

• Scan the market independently
• Discover opportunities independently
• Evaluate opportunities independently
• Decide independently
• Audit itself
• Learn from its own decisions
• Improve future discovery quality
• Improve future decision quality

No human intervention should be required for normal operation.

====================================================================

# PRIMARY DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Rule #1

AEIM is NOT a stock screener.

AEIM is an autonomous reasoning system.

------------------------------------------------------------

Rule #2

The stock scanner website is a completely separate product.

The scanner may continue to exist.

It may continue generating signals.

However...

AEIM must NEVER require scanner signals.

AEIM must be fully operational if the stock scanner is shut down.

------------------------------------------------------------

Rule #3

AEIM must discover opportunities directly from Polygon.

Polygon is the primary source of market intelligence.

Every opportunity should originate from AEIM's own discovery engine.

------------------------------------------------------------

Rule #4

AEIM should continuously improve.

It should become better at:

Finding stocks

Ranking stocks

Managing risk

Understanding macro

Understanding leadership

Understanding corrections

Finding tactical dip buys

Avoiding broken trends

Calibrating confidence

Managing portfolio exposure

# AEIM CORE PHILOSOPHY

AEIM should think exactly like a professional
portfolio manager.

Before asking

"Should I buy this stock?"

AEIM first asks

"What kind of market am I trading?"

Then

"Which sectors are strongest?"

Then

"Which industries lead?"

Then

"Which stocks are highest quality?"

Then

"Is today's weakness an opportunity?"

Then

"What is the appropriate position size?"

Only then

may AEIM approve a trade.
